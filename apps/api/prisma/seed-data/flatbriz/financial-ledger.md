## Per-Flat Dr/Cr Running Balance

<!-- tags: benefit:transparent_ledger -->
Each flat has its own ledger of debit (Dr) and credit (Cr) entries. A maintenance bill raised for
the flat posts as a debit; a payment received posts as a credit. The running balance after each
entry is stored, so both the resident and the building admin can see, at any point in time, exactly
what's owed and how the current balance was arrived at — not just a final "amount due" number with
no trail behind it.

## Automatic Bill Generation

<!-- tags: benefit:transparent_ledger -->
Building admins configure a maintenance billing cycle once (base maintenance per flat, sinking
fund contribution, due date, late fee rule). From then on, bills are generated automatically each
period for every flat according to that configuration, rather than a treasurer manually preparing
a new spreadsheet each month.

## Corpus Fund Management

<!-- tags: benefit:corpus_fund_management -->
Sinking-fund/corpus contributions are tracked as their own component of each bill, separate from
regular maintenance, so the society's corpus balance is visible and auditable on its own rather
than blended into general maintenance collections. This makes it straightforward to report the
corpus fund's position at an AGM without a manual reconciliation exercise.

## Late Fees & Advance Payments

<!-- tags: benefit:transparent_ledger -->
Late fee rules configured by the admin apply automatically to overdue bills. If a resident pays
more than their current bill (an advance payment), the surplus is tracked and available to offset
future bills rather than being lost track of.

## Expense Tracking

<!-- tags: benefit:transparent_ledger, benefit:corpus_fund_management -->
Building outgoings (society expenses) can be tracked alongside collections, giving the committee a
single place to reconcile what came in against what went out — useful for AGM reporting and for
answering resident questions about where maintenance money is going.

## Audit Trail

<!-- tags: benefit:transparent_ledger -->
Every ledger entry (bill, payment, correction) is timestamped and attributable, so if a resident or
auditor asks "why does this flat's balance look like this," the full history is there rather than a
single net figure.
